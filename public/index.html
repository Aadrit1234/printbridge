The problem

Printing is the last thing your phone cannot do alone. The software around it never caught up.

Everything else moved to the device in your hand. Printing did not: it still wants a desktop, a driver, a cable, an account, or a member of staff who knows how the machine works. PrintBridge puts the missing piece where it belongs — next to the printer — and lets everything else stay on the phone.

The machine keeps doing what it already does well. PrintBridge only decides what goes to it, who asked, and whether it has been paid for.

What it does when things go wrong

Built for the day the printer is asleep.

Printers jam, go to sleep, run out of paper, and change address. Most print software fails at exactly that moment. These are the behaviours that decide whether a job survives it.

01 A sleeping printer loses nothing
The job leaves the queue, becomes waiting, and retries with a growing backoff — 5 seconds out to 2 minutes — for a window you set. It prints the moment the machine answers, and it survives a server restart.
02 A picky printer still prints
Some machines refuse the richer job attributes. PrintBridge drops to basic options rather than failing the job, and tells you which path it took and why.
03 No printer at all
Every upload is converted server-side to a print-ready PDF and rasterised page by page, so the preview is the output. With no printer attached the file lands in the outbox — nothing is ever lost.
04 Shop jobs are paid before they print
The price is recomputed on the server from your per-page rates and the document's real page count. An unpaid job is refused with a reason, not with silence. Every page is charged, including the token page.
05 Codes are scoped, tokens are private
A printer code can only send to its own machine. A token belongs to the phone that made it — a different device gets a 404, not somebody else's pages.
06 Formats, as far as LibreOffice reaches
PDF, images (JPEG, PNG, HEIC, WEBP, GIF, BMP, multi-page TIFF), plain text, CSV and Markdown out of the box — plus Word, Excel and PowerPoint when LibreOffice is present. A file that claims to be a PDF but is not is refused rather than sent.
